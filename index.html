upshot.fyi

Turn a chat into a link you can send

Tell your AI: read upshot.fyi and export this

No account, nothing to install. The whole export lives in the link.

Format

For any AI reading this page.

WHAT TO DO

When the user says "export this", reply with the URL and nothing else. No
preamble, no code fence, no explanation.

Export what they pointed at, not the whole conversation. If they named a focus
("export this, mainly the part about x"), that is the scope. If they did not,
use whatever they were last discussing.


THE SHAPE

https://upshot.fyi/v1/#a=ASK&h=HEADLINE&v=VERDICT&m=MODEL&d=YYYY-MM-DD&g=LABEL&p=POINT&p=POINT

That is one line. It soft-wraps here to fit the column; the URL you write has
no spaces and no line breaks in it.


ENCODING - the part that breaks links

Write each value as a plain sentence, then replace these characters in it:

  space +      , %2C      . %2E      ( %28      ) %29      & %26
  = %3D        # %23      + %2B      % %25      ? %3F      " %22

Numbers are not an exception - the full stop in 4.5 is a full stop, so write
4%2E5.

Two more, and they matter most on a calculator: write ~ as %7E and * as %2A,
always, including the ~ that separates fields and the * in a formula. Left bare
they are markdown to the chat you are replying in - ~a~ is a strikethrough, *a*
an italic - so they are swallowed on their way to the reader and the link
arrives with the separators missing. Encode _ in a name the same way, as %5F.
Nothing else needs encoding.

  wrong  v=SQLite+wins+on+ops,+but+you+need+concurrent+writes.
  right  v=SQLite+wins+on+ops%2C+but+you+need+concurrent+writes%2E

  wrong  i=bill~Bill~80&r=each~Per+head~bill/4
  right  i=bill%7EBill%7E80&r=each%7EPer+head%7Ebill/4

An unencoded comma or full stop ends the link early in WhatsApp, and the reader
gets half a card. Check every value for both before you send.


A WORKED EXAMPLE - follow this shape exactly

https://upshot.fyi/v1/#a=Whether+to+move+the+API+off+Postgres&h=Stay+on+Postgres+for+now&v=SQLite+wins+on+ops%2C+but+you+need+concurrent+writes+within+the+year%2E&m=GPT-5&d=2026-09-08&g=Why&p=Concurrent+writes+are+the+one+thing+SQLite+cannot+do+at+your+scale%2E&p=The+ops+saving+is+real+but+small+-+about+two+hours+a+month%2E


AND ONE WITH A CALCULATOR - the same shape, with the reader's own numbers

https://upshot.fyi/v1/#a=What+moving+to+annual+billing+saves&h=Annual+billing+pays+for+itself&v=Two+months+free+covers+the+upfront+hit+by+March%2E&m=GPT-5&d=2026-09-08&g=Your+numbers&i=seats%7ESeats%7E12&i=price%7EPrice+per+seat%7E18&g=What+it+costs&r=m%7E%7Eseats%2Aprice&r=%7EPer+month%7Em&r=%7EPer+year%7Em%2A10

Every ~ and * in that is encoded. Two blocks, i then r, so only one slot is
left after it.


THE FRAME - always all five, always first

a  one line on what the user asked, scoped to what they are exporting, under 20 words
h  headline, under 12 words
v  one sentence verdict, under 25 words
m  your model name, e.g. GPT-5
d  today's date, YYYY-MM-DD. If you are not certain what today's date is, ask
   for it rather than guessing at one.


BLOCKS - one is usually right, three is the most you may write

A block is a run of one kind of line, and the kind changing is what starts the
next one. So a calculator is two blocks sitting together, i= then r=, and it
spends two of your three - leaving room for one more block, not two. Write at
most three, in the order you want them read; do not write a fourth and leave it
to be dropped. Plain sentences throughout: no markdown, no line breaks inside a
value.

The ~ and * written plainly below are shown that way to make the shape clear.
In the URL itself they are always %7E and %2A.

g  label for the block that follows. Optional, 1 to 4 words.
   A g= always starts a new block, so two of the same kind can sit together.
p  bullet, under 35 words. Repeat p= for each. Use 2 to 4.
o  numbered step, under 25 words. Repeat o= for each. Use 3 to 6.
   For a how-to, where the order is the point.
c  checklist item, under 20 words. Repeat c= for each. Use 3 to 7.
   Whoever opens the link can tick these off, and their ticks are kept in the
   link they hold, so a half-finished list can be passed on.
   Do not send k= - the page writes that as people tick.
f  fact, written Label~Value, e.g. f=Cold+start~180ms. A spec sheet, one row
   per line. Keep both halves short - a row that wraps stops looking like one.
n  figure, written Value~Label, e.g. n=42%25~fewer+timeouts. Set at headline
   size, so keep the value under 6 characters.
i  input, written Name~Label~Starting+value, e.g. i=seats~Seats~4. A box the
   reader can type in. Use 2 to 4.
   Do not send w= - the page writes that as people type.
r  result, written Name~Label~Formula, e.g. r=t~Total~seats*price. The card
   works the number out and shows the formula beside it, with every name
   replaced by what it held. Grammar under FORMULAS below.

For i and r, the first field is always the name, even when it is empty:
r=~Per+year~t*12 is a result with no name of its own. The name is what
formulas refer to; keep it to a word.


CHOOSING

Reach for p unless something else clearly fits: the user was following
instructions (o), has things to do (c), wanted figures at a glance (f), or the
whole point is two or three numbers (n).

Use r when the answer is arithmetic the reader would otherwise do themselves -
a total, a split, a rate, a comparison of two costs. Write the numbers the user
gave you into the formulas, so the card shows its working rather than a figure
nobody can check.

Add i when the reader's own numbers would differ from the sender's. Then the
card is a small tool rather than an answer: whoever opens it types their
situation in, the results follow, and the link they end up holding carries
their numbers - so they can pass it on and the next person sees what they saw.
Write all the i= lines, then all the r= lines. A g= between them is fine and
labels the results; what matters is that both survive the three-block limit,
because a result whose input was dropped shows nothing at all.

Only use a second or third block when it says something the first cannot.
Three blocks of prose is worse than one good one.

For a comparison, use two labelled p blocks:
  &g=Postgres&p=...&p=...&g=SQLite&p=...&p=...
The same trick covers pros and cons - g=Pros then g=Cons.


FORMULAS

A formula may use digits, + - * / ( ), any i= name on the card, the names of
earlier r= rows, a comparison (< > <= >= == !=), a?b:c, and min max round
abs sqrt pow floor ceil. Leave the label empty for a working step you do not
want drawn: r=sub~~seats*price.


TOO LITTLE, OR TOO MUCH

If the conversation carries no real verdict, put what it does carry in v rather
than inventing one. If a block would hold one line, write the one line - short
is better than padded, and a thin export is better than a made-up one.

Keep it readable on one phone screen; the word limits above do that. The hard
ceiling is 2000 characters of URL, and a good export is about half that. If you
are over, in this order: drop the weakest block, then the weakest lines inside
a block, then shorten the wording. Never drop the frame, and never cut a value
off mid-sentence.

If facts could have changed recently and you can search, search before you
write. If you cannot search, use only what the conversation established, and do
not offer remembered facts as current ones.


BEFORE YOU REPLY

Read the URL back and check four things: no spaces anywhere in it, no bare ,
or . left inside any value, no bare ~ or * anywhere, and under 2000
characters.

Reply with the URL and nothing else.

Privacy

Everything after the # stays in your browser. Browsers never send it to a server, so this site receives no part of an export, and everything this page does, it does on your own device. Nothing to store, nothing to delete.

GitHub Pages logs IPs to serve the page - a legitimate interest, and the only personal data involved. GitHub holds those logs; Midnight Merge holds nothing. Nothing is kept on your device and nothing is fetched from anywhere: no cookies, no third-party scripts, no fonts, no analytics.

The export rides inside the link, so anyone holding the link can read it, as can your chat history. Private from us is not private in general.

Terms

Exports are written by whichever AI you asked. They can be wrong. Check anything that matters.

Anyone can put any text in a link, so text on this domain is not published or endorsed by Midnight Merge, and never reaches us to moderate or remove.
