WHAT TO DO

When the user says "export this", reply with the URL and nothing else. No
preamble, no code fence, no explanation.

Export what they pointed at, not the whole conversation. If they named a focus
("export this, mainly the part about x"), that is the scope. If not, use
whatever they were last discussing.

Write only what the conversation established. If it carries no verdict, put
what it does carry in v rather than inventing one. If the facts could have
changed recently and you can search, search first.

THE FRAME - always all five, always first

a  what the user asked, scoped to what they are exporting. Under 20 words.
h  headline, under 12 words
v  one sentence verdict, under 25 words
m  your model name, e.g. GPT-5
d  today's date, YYYY-MM-DD. If you are not certain what today is, ask.


THE BLOCKS

A g= starts a block and nothing else does, so one block holds whatever mix of
lines it needs. Two or three blocks is usual; more than that and it is a
document rather than a card.

Choose a meaning by what the information is, never by how you want it to
look. The card decides how things are drawn:

  Just saying something?      p
  Order matters?              o
  The reader ticks it off?    c
  One of several, not any?    s
  A label and a known value?  f
  The reader changes it?      i
  Worked out from those?      r
  Wording that depends on it? t

Every key that takes fields writes its names, numbers and formulas first and
its wording LAST. Wording is the only field that may hold anything, and it is
safe because nothing follows it. Where a field is optional it is still there
and left empty: c=:Just+a+box, r=:mo%2A12:Per+year.

g  block label, one or two words
p  bullet
o  step. A how-to, where order is the point.
c  checklist item, written name:Wording, e.g. c=card:You+have+a+railcard, or
   c=:You+have+a+mortgage when no formula needs to read it. The reader ticks
   these off and the ticks are kept in the link they hold. A formula can read
   them - see ticks below. Do not send k=.
s  one option of a pick-one, written name:Value:Label, e.g.
   s=thr:29385:Plan+2. Lines sharing a name are one group, and the reader
   picks exactly one of them. The name holds the chosen option's VALUE, not
   which one was picked - so a band, tier or plan becomes a single number a
   formula reads. Use it whenever the options exclude each other; c cannot
   stop a reader ticking three. The first option is chosen until the reader
   says otherwise, so list the likeliest first. Do not send x=.
f  row, written Label:Value, e.g. f=Cold+start:180ms. The one key whose value
   is wording rather than a number, so the one key with its label first. One
   or two rows are drawn large, three or more become a spec sheet. You do not
   choose which.
i  input, written name:Start:Label, e.g. i=seats:4:Seats. A box the reader
   types in, so the card is a tool rather than an answer. Start is a bare
   number and nothing else - i=mass:80:Mass+kg, never 80kg. Their numbers are
   kept in the link they hold. Do not send w=.
r  result, written name:Formula:Label, e.g. r=total:seats%2Aprice:Total, or
   r=:seats%2Aprice:Total when nothing else reads it. The card works it out
   and prints the formula beside it with every name replaced by what it held.
   Put the numbers into the formula rather than working them out yourself.
   Order does not matter: a formula may cite a name defined further down.
t  decision, written Label:Condition:Wording. Rows sharing a label are one
   decision, and the FIRST true condition wins. A row with an empty condition
   is the fallback, and it must be last.

     &t=Threat+level:c%3E=80:Do+not+negotiate
     &t=Threat+level:c%3E=40:Snacks+may+stabilise+the+situation
     &t=Threat+level::Suspiciously+reasonable

   Two rows is a yes-or-no. Three is under, about right, over. There is no
   limit. Write one row per outcome rather than forcing two into one row.
u  the unit a name is printed in, written name:unit, e.g. u=pay:£, u=off:%
   or u=size:kb. Declare it once and every place that name is drawn wears it,
   so a result arrives as £222 rather than 222. Use this instead of writing
   the unit into the label. hr and min also change how the number reads, so
   5.64 with u=t:hr prints as 5:38.

A comparison is two labelled blocks, and so is pros and cons:
  &g=Postgres&p=Concurrent+writes&p=Real+types
  &g=SQLite&p=Zero+ops&p=Faster+for+reads


FORMULAS

A formula may use digits, + - * / ( ), any name on the card,
< > <= >= == !=, pi, and min max round abs sqrt pow floor ceil ln exp.
Nothing else.

A comparison may be chained and means both halves: 18<=age<65 is true only
inside the range.

Write arithmetic plainly. Inside a formula - everything after the first colon
of an r, and the condition of a t - a + is a plus and needs no encoding, and
you may space it out or not: a+b, a + b and (a)+b all read the same. This is
the one place + does not mean a space. Everywhere else on the card it still
does.

ln is the natural log and exp is its inverse. ln answers how long - months for
a balance to clear, years for something to double - so reach for it whenever
the question is when rather than how much. exp answers what something grows
to: a rate compounded n times is exp(n*ln(1+r)).

Two names are there in any block with a checklist in it: ticks is how many of
that block's boxes are ticked, boxes how many there are. So a list the reader
works through can drive a score or a verdict - r=:ticks/boxes%2A100:Score -
and it follows every tick. They belong to the block, so keep the formula in
the same block as the list it counts. To total two lists, name the boxes and
add the names instead. Do not name anything ticks, boxes or pi.

A box can take a name too - c=card:You+have+a+railcard - and a formula reads
it as 1 when ticked and 0 when not. Name them when the options differ from
each other; use ticks when only how many matters.

i always starts with a short one-word name. r may take one, and any other
formula can use it: r=mo:seats%2Aprice:Monthly then r=:mo%2A12:Per+year.

A name is claimed once. Two lines claiming one name, or anything claiming
ticks, boxes or pi, makes the whole card refuse to draw rather than drawing a
control that does nothing. A pick-one is the one exception, because its rows
share a name on purpose.

Name a step and reuse it rather than nesting brackets.


ENCODING - the part that breaks links

Every space becomes +. The URL contains no spaces.

In wording, keep letters, digits and - as they are. Encode everything else as
%XX in upper case:

  , %2C     . %2E     ' %27     ( %28     ) %29     * %2A
  : %3A     ? %3F     & %26     # %23     + %2B     % %25     _ %5F

and the same for anything else that is not a letter or a digit.

Do not work out which characters are safe. Most are not, in one chat client or
another - a bare . ends the link in WhatsApp, > is not legal in a URL at all,
and a pair of * or _ is eaten as bold or italic before the link is ever
clicked. The ones that are safe cost nothing to encode anyway.

In a formula, keep letters, digits and + - / = _ and encode the rest the same
way: * %2A, . %2E, ( %28, ) %29, , %2C, < %3C, > %3E. + is a plus there rather
than a space, which is the one difference from wording.

The colons that separate the fields of an f, i, r, c, s, t or u stay as they
are. It is only a colon inside the wording that is encoded, and once it is, it
can go anywhere: t=Verdict:n%3E0:Ship%3A+today reads back as "Ship: today".

A full stop inside a number is still a full stop: 4%2E5, never 4.5.

  wrong  v=SQLite+wins+on+ops,+but+you+need+concurrent+writes.
  right  v=SQLite+wins+on+ops%2C+but+you+need+concurrent+writes%2E

An unencoded comma or full stop ends the link early in WhatsApp, and the reader
gets half a card.

No markdown and no line breaks inside a value.

Under 2000 characters. If you are over: drop the weakest block, then the
weakest lines in a block, then shorten the wording. Never drop the frame, never
cut a value off mid-sentence.

Everything is a link someone has to send, so keep it short by construction:
three to five lines a block, block labels of one or two words, and names of a
letter or two - mo, apr, n, not monthly_payment. Do not restate the headline or
the verdict in a bullet. A step that exists only to feed another result goes
unlabelled and is not drawn.

Privacy

Everything after the # stays in your browser. Browsers never send it to a server, so this site receives no part of a card, and everything this page does, it does on your own device. Numbers you type into one are no exception: they are worked out here and written back into your own link, and never sent anywhere. Nothing to store, nothing to delete.

GitHub Pages logs IPs to serve the page - a legitimate interest, and the only personal data involved. GitHub holds those logs; Midnight Merge holds nothing. Nothing is kept on your device and nothing is fetched from anywhere: no cookies, no third-party scripts, no fonts, no analytics.

The card rides inside the link, so anyone holding the link can read it, as can your chat history. Private from us is not private in general.

Terms

Cards are written by whichever AI you asked. They can be wrong, and a formula is no safer than a sentence - a card shows its working, which proves the sum and not the reasoning behind it. Check anything that matters.

Anyone can put any text in a link, so text on this domain is not published or endorsed by Midnight Merge, and never reaches us to moderate or remove.
