# Marketing

Working notes. Nothing here is decided.

## What it is

Upshot turns an AI answer into a card you can send. No account, no install,
either end. The card is the link.

**The noun is a card.** A calculator is one thing a card can be, next to a
checklist, a spec sheet, a set of figures, a set of steps. Never name the
category after its narrowest instance.

## The claim, ranked

1. **Nothing reaches us.** Everything lives after the `#`, which browsers
   never send to a server. Not a policy - the way the web works.
2. **No account, no install, on either end.** The receiver taps a link. That
   is where most sharing tools die.
3. **Any AI, zero integration.** No SDK, no plugin, no key. A model reads one
   page and writes a URL, so it works with whatever ships next month.
4. **A tool, not a transcript.** The card answers for whoever opens it.
5. **Their changes ride in the link.** Stateful, no backend.
6. **It cannot render ugly.** The model picks meaning, the card picks looks.
7. **Links do not rot.** `/v1/` renders every link ever written against it.

## Lines to test

- The link that can't phone home.
- Your AI's answer, as something you can use.
- Send the answer, not the transcript.

## Keep out of the copy

- "Private" with no mechanism. Anyone with the link can read it, and it sits
  in your chat history. *Private from us, structurally* is the honest claim
  and the stronger one.
- The encoding work. It is why this functions, not a reason to want it.

## Who

Unresolved, and it decides everything below. Candidates:

- People who already talk to an AI daily and hit "how do I send this"
- Anyone who would otherwise paste a screenshot into WhatsApp
- Devs, as the first wave, because they will read a spec for fun

## Open

- What the demo is - a bill splitter proves it in one tap, but a demo that
  looks like a calculator may pin the category to calculators
- Does the AI say the name out loud, and can we live with it if it does not
- Whether `upshot` competes for the word or owns it
